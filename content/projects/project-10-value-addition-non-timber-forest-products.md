## Project Summary
Sensitizing and training forest-dwelling farmers about commercial value addition and marketing of wild NTFP harvests.

## Project Historical Overview & Operational Methodology
Nagaland's pristine forests abound in wild culinary herbs, wild honey, natural edible mushrooms, wild apple, star anise, wild cinnamon, and herbal bark. Historically, these valuable products were gathered for meager local sales or spoiled due to lack of processing.

NEPED collaborated with CCS NIAM Jaipur to train forest gatherers in hygienic solar drying, vacuum sealing, moisture testing, and commercial branding.

## Key Strategic Mandates & Objectives
1. Identify high-value commercial NTFP species across different altitude zones in Nagaland.
2. Equip forest-dwelling families with solar dehydrators, stainless steel processing tools, and vacuum sealers.
3. Eliminate post-harvest fungal spoilage through scientific grading and packaging.
4. Link village NTFP collectors directly with organic gourmet food and Ayurvedic pharmaceutical brands.

## Major Milestones & Institutional Deliverables
* Over 400 wild produce gatherers trained across 20 biodiversity-rich villages.
* Established 5 village solar-drying clusters for wild apple chips, wild garlic, and indigenous turmeric.
* Developed retail packaging and food-grade lab certifications for 8 wild forest products.
* Increased village gate prices for raw forest harvests by over 45% through simple post-harvest value addition.
